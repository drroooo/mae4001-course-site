    MAE4001 Convergent Media and Users
    (융합미디어와 이용자)¶

    Quick Info

    • Semester: 2026 Fall · 3 Credits · Undergraduate
    • Class Hours: Tue/Thu 09:00–10:15
    • Office: GA702 · Office Hours: Mon, Wed, Thu 12:00–14:00 or by appointment
    • E-mail: mjrheu@sogang.ac.kr

    Ⅰ. Course Overview¶

    Communication technologies powerfully shape how we attend, learn, communicate, make decisions, and relate to others. Their impact is not accidental: although it may be difficult to discern, it is a product of careful and deliberate design based on decades of socio-psychological research findings.

    This course aims to open your eyes to the social-psychological mechanisms and technological affordances underlying user experiences across various media, including social media, mobile apps, games, VR, and AI-powered technologies. These technologies increasingly converge, allowing users to navigate an interconnected media landscape rather than engaging with each in isolation. Understanding users in this environment requires attention both to the distinctive affordances of each medium and to the psychological principles that operate across media boundaries.

    The course follows a project-based, learning-by-doing model. Using design-thinking methods, students will identify a meaningful user problem, gather and synthesize user evidence, develop a theory-informed technological solution, and create and evaluate a functional prototype demonstrating one critical user pathway.

    Ⅱ. Course Objectives¶

    Knowledge After taking class, students will be able to understand the socio-psychological mechanisms and principles underlying how people engage with media and information technologies; explain how the distinctive affordances of different media shape user experience and possible design outcomes; and critically examine intended effects and unintended consequences on individuals and and society as a whole, including their ethical issues in technology design.

    Skills Be able to connect everyday media-use experiences to theoretical explanations and concepts covered in class, and apply social/psychological principles to the design of technology based on the insights darwn from scientific user research.

    Attitudes Approach technology design with curiosity, ethical awareness, and respect for user well-being and autonomy; and participate responsibly and constructively in collaborative project work.

    Ⅲ. Course Format¶

    • Lectures
    • Class Discussion
    • Design-thinking activities and project workshops
    • Team project presentations

    The semester project will be developed progressively during class. Teams will maintain a shared Project Process Folder containing interview materials, affinity maps, ideation records, scenarios, task flows, prototype materials, and feedback records. Workshop artifacts may be submitted as Miro exports, photographs, diagrams, screenshots, or other appropriate formats.

    Ⅳ. Requirements & Grading¶

    Assessment Components¶

    • Class Participation and Professionalism — 10%
    • Midterm: Theory-Based App Analysis — 15%
    • Class Project
      • Deliverable 1: User Problem Brief — 20%
      • Deliverable 2: Theory-Based Design Proposal — 20%
      • Deliverable 3: Functional Prototype and Design Case — 20%
    • Final Presentation and Peer Evaluation — 15%

    Class Project Deliverables¶

    • Deliverable 1: User Research and Problem Definition Brief
    • Deliverable 2: Theory-Based Design Proposal
    • Deliverable 3: Functional Prototype and Design Case

    Ⅴ. Course Policies¶

    Communication

    The instructor aims to respond to e-mails within 48 hours on weekdays. Students are responsible for checking Cybercampus and e-mail regularly for course announcements and updates.

    Late Work

    Assignments are due at the specified date and time. Late work will be accepted only when an extension has been discussed with the instructor in advance, such as in cases of illness or emergency. Work submitted late without prior communication may receive no credit.

    Academic Honesty and AI Use

    Plagiarism, self-plagiarism, data fabrication, collusion, and cheating are serious violations of academic integrity and will be addressed according to university policy. Generative AI may be used when specifically permitted for brainstorming, research support, and prototyping. Students remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, and intellectual contribution of submitted work and must disclose AI use as instructed. Fabricated user evidence, interview data, sources, or testing results are prohibited.

    Teamwork

    All team members are expected to contribute throughout the project. Individual contribution statements and confidential peer evaluations may be used to assess individual participation in team deliverables.
