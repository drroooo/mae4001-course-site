What counts as communication technology?

Working definition

Technologies that create, process, organize, transmit, recommend, or respond to information and social signals.

  • Devices and sensors
  • Apps, platforms, and interfaces
  • Algorithms, agents, and connected services

Look beyond the screen

Convergent media

Design connects features to lived experience

Analytical chain
Step 1

Feature

A concrete design element or system behavior

Step 2

Possibility

What becomes easier, visible, persistent, or expected

Step 3

Experience

What the user notices, feels, thinks, or does

Step 4

Outcome

Benefits, costs, tradeoffs, and wider effects

One feature can reorganize a social situation

Example
Typing…

Design possibility

The interface makes another person's ongoing activity visible.

Possible experiences

Anticipation, reassurance, impatience, pressure—or no meaningful effect.

Intended effect is not the same as experienced impact

Design consequence

Intended

Goal
Help people notice relevant updates.
Feature
Push notification.
Expected benefit
Timely awareness and response.

Experienced

Possible benefit
Useful reminder or social connection.
Possible cost
Interruption, urgency, or anxiety.
Open question
For whom, when, and under what conditions?

Five domains of possible impact

Prompts—not conclusions
AttentionWhat becomes salient, interrupted, or difficult to ignore?01
EmotionWhat feelings arise during or after use?02
JudgmentWhat seems credible, urgent, normal, or valuable?03
RelationshipsHow does interaction, presence, or obligation change?04
BehaviorWhat action becomes easier, repeated, delayed, or avoided?05

Separate what you know from what you infer

Evidence discipline
1

Observation

“I reopened the app four times while studying.”

2

Interpretation

“The notifications seemed to create urgency.”

3

Causal claim

“Notifications caused my loss of concentration.”

Day 2In-class activity

Technology
Impact Map

Trace one technology-use episode from feature to possible impact.

Your goal

Individual map

Make one everyday technology experience visible enough to question.

  • Choose one specific episode—not your entire day.
  • Trace a design feature to a perceived experience or effect.
  • Mark what is intended, unintended, or uncertain.
  • End with one investigable question.

Build the map from left to right

Map structure
1

Episode

User, technology, context, and purpose

2

Feature

The specific design element or system behavior

3

Experience

Attention, emotion, judgment, relationship, or behavior

4

Question

An uncertainty worth investigating

Example: checking messages before bed

Model map
Episode

Before bed

Feature

Unread count

A visible number signals unfinished messages.

Experience

Urgency

Question

What is uncertain?

Would hiding the count change when I stop?

Activity flow

About 45 minutes
StageWhat to do
1Individual mapping · 15 minChoose an episode and build the first version.
2Pair review · 10 minFind one intended effect, one unintended effect, and one uncertain claim.
3Pattern sharing · 10 minCompare recurring features, impacts, and differences.
4Revision · 10 minRevise the map and write one investigable question.

During pair review, look for both value and cost
